Where this came from

The Villain Lab grew out of a 2011 episode of This American Life, "The Psychopath Test." It's a class project about AI agents, and a small lesson about how easy it is to see what you're looking for.

How the radio staff scored

Before getting their results, the staff of a public radio show voted on who would score highest. Most picked Rosa, a producer who admitted to stealing a teacher's answer manual in junior high. A few picked June, who had been a rebellious teenager with a couple of run-ins with the law. One person picked the host, Ivan, whose coworkers find him a little detached. Nora voted for herself, just to win.

Then the forensic psychologist, Dr. Barnes, came in with his notes. His first observation got a laugh: the group was far too neurotic to be psychopaths, and half of them nearly cried during the questions. The scores: every single person got a zero. Even Rosa. She hadn't sold the stolen answers; she gave them away free so her classmates would do well. And everyone who had done something wrong looked back on it with regret. What they all had, he said, was empathy, the opposite of psychopathy.

The lesson: real psychopathy isn't a few bad moments from your teens. It's a complete lack of conscience over a whole lifetime, and only a trained clinician can judge it, with an interview and checked records.

Source: This American Life #436, "The Psychopath Test" (May 27, 2011). Names changed.

How the panel works

The episode describes research by Daniel Murrie and colleagues: when forensic experts were told they'd been hired by the prosecution, they scored the same case files as more dangerous than experts told they'd been hired by the defense. Researchers call it adversarial allegiance.

The Villain Panel turns that finding into a game. Two AI agents get the same fictional character and the same eight trait themes. Vera Stone argues for the prosecution, Felix Hart for the defense, and Judge Okafor shows where they split and why. The eight themes (Charm Offensive, Main Character Energy, Con Artistry, Remorse Meter, Cold Heart, Thrill Hunger, Short Fuse and Track Record) are our own invention. They are not taken from any clinical test, and the Villain Index out of 100 is not a clinical score.

Two extras: Chase Sterling, the Spin Doctor, recasts every dark trait as a leadership strength, a nod to a CEO in the episode who did exactly that. Hope Rivera compares a character's beginning with their ending, because the episode's hardest story is about a test that can't see change.

The rules

  • Fictional characters only. The panel refuses real people, including people you know.
  • For entertainment only. Nothing here is a diagnosis, and nobody here is a clinician.
  • Original content. We never reproduce the real psychopathy checklist, which is a licensed clinical instrument.

Privacy

  • The quiz runs entirely in your browser. Your answers are never sent anywhere and are never saved. Refreshing clears them.
  • A live Villain Panel sends the character's name, the agents' notes on that character and the class password (if one is set) to our server, which passes the name and notes to Anthropic's Claude AI for a ruling. Please don't type personal details into the name box. The archive villains and the quiz send nothing.
  • If anything here makes you worried about yourself or someone you know, a doctor or counselor is the right person to talk to. No quiz or app can tell you that.
  • No accounts, no cookies, no analytics, no tracking scripts, no outside fonts.
