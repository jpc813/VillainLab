# Adversarial allegiance: why two experts can score the same person differently

This is the research behind the app's headline feature, the **Villain Panel**.

## The finding

**"Adversarial allegiance"** is the pull for forensic experts to reach conclusions that favor the side that hired them. (Murrie & Boccaccini, AAFS 2014.)

### Field study (2009)
Murrie and colleagues reviewed real sexually-violent-predator cases where evaluators hired by opposing sides each scored the same offender on risk tools, including the PCL-R. Agreement was much weaker than in research settings, and the differences leaned toward each expert's side. (Murrie, Boccaccini, Turner, Meeks, Woods & Tussey, 2009, *Psychology, Public Policy, and Law*, 15, 19–53, doi:10.1037/a0014897.) The episode cites this line of work: average gaps of around 8 points, sometimes 10–20.

### Experiment (2013)
To rule out "lawyers just pick friendly experts," the team **paid 108 forensic psychologists and psychiatrists** to review the **same offender case files**, telling some they were consulting for the prosecution and others for the defense. Those who believed they worked for the prosecution assigned **higher risk scores**; those who believed they worked for the defense assigned **lower** ones. (Murrie, Boccaccini, Guarnera & Rufino, 2013, "Are Forensic Experts Biased by the Side That Retained Them?", *Psychological Science*.)

Murrie said the team was surprised by how easy the effect was to find, and that most expert witnesses believe they are objective.

## How the app uses this

- **Prosecution Rater** and **Defense Rater** get the *same* evidence about a fictional character and the *same* trait themes, but opposite instructions about who hired them.
- The **Judge** shows the two scores side by side, points to the specific traits where they split, and explains *why*: which evidence each side emphasized or downplayed.
- The UI should make the gap the star of the show: "Same character. Same evidence. A 14-point disagreement."
- A short explainer under every verdict links back here and to the Resources page.
