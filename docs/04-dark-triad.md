# The Dark Triad

Useful background for the Literary Analyst, the Judge and the Spin Doctor: not every villain is a psychopath.

## What it is

The **Dark Triad** was introduced by psychologists **Delroy Paulhus and Kevin Williams** (University of British Columbia) in **2002**. It groups three "overlapping but distinct" socially aversive personality traits that fall within the normal (non-clinical) range:

| Trait | Core flavor | Villain shorthand |
|---|---|---|
| **Machiavellianism** | Strategic manipulation, cynicism, long-game scheming | The schemer |
| **Narcissism** (subclinical) | Grandiosity, entitlement, need for admiration | The egoist |
| **Psychopathy** (subclinical) | Callousness, impulsivity, thrill-seeking, low empathy | The cold predator |

The three share a common core sometimes called **callous manipulation**: disregard for others' feelings for personal gain. Of the three, psychopathy is generally considered the most malevolent. Researchers have since proposed adding **everyday sadism** (a "Dark Tetrad").

## Why it matters for the app

- The Judge's verdict should be able to say: *"High on Machiavellianism, moderate narcissism, low on the psychopathy cluster. A schemer, not a psychopath."* That's more interesting and more accurate than yes/no.
- Many famous villains are better described as narcissists or schemers; many antiheroes act out of loyalty, fear or trauma, which points *away* from psychopathy.
- Critics note the three traits overlap heavily in measurement, so the app should present them as flavors, not precise diagnoses.
